Broker Opinion of Value · Comparative Market Analysis
634 Newark Avenue
Jersey City, NJ 07306 · Journal Square · Newark Ave Corridor

634 Newark Avenue (Block 8101, Lot 30) is a 4,792 SF lot, roughly 26' wide by 185' deep, in Zone 5 (Commercial Main Street) of the Journal Square 2060 Redevelopment Plan. It is improved with a three-story mixed-use building of 3,246 SF: ground-floor retail with two market-rate apartments above. The building covers only the front ~42' of the lot, leaving the rear three-quarters undeveloped.

We estimate value at $1.75M–$1.95M as a condominium development site, with a recommended list price of $1,950,000. Grid's schematic massing supports a five-story, ~12,800 GSF elevator building with 10 units, roughly four times today's floor area. Pricing is anchored to the August 2026 sale of 361 Central Avenue, an inline lot bought by a builder for a 14-unit condo project at $185,714 per door. Our range runs from $175,000 to $195,000 per door, 94% to 105% of that basis.

Value Drivers
Site Highlights
Under-Built Lot
The existing building uses about 42' of a 185'-deep lot. A buyer is paying for land that can hold roughly four times today's 3,246 SF.
10-Unit Elevator Concept
Eight upper-floor units plus two rear garden residences over a continuous cellar, sized for a boutique condo offering. Zone 5 projects are exempt from the affordable set-aside, and no parking is permitted on lots under 60' wide.
Ground-Zero Condo Site at the PATH
A short walk to the Journal Square PATH station and bus terminal, Jersey City's central transit hub. That location sells condos: a one-seat ride to Manhattan is what end buyers pay for.
Upside Not Priced In
The 10-unit program assumes upper floors stop 85' from Newark Ave. If Zone 5 allows deeper floor plates, or the rear of the lot gains frontage on the planned Central Avenue Extension, the unit count could rise further.

Comparable Support · Marketing Thesis
A Condo-Only Offering, Priced Off 361 Central

We will market 634 Newark exclusively to condo developers on the development return. Pricing it on a cap rate, or against buildings that traded on cash flow, leaves money on the table.

Objections we are ready for
  • “It's overpriced.” It is priced at what a builder just paid per door.
  • “The cap rate doesn't work.” It's not a hold; the return comes at condo sell-out.
  • “Taxes are rising.” A builder carries them only through approvals and construction, and existing rent offsets the carry.
